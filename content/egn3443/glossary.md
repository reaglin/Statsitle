---
title: Glossary
---

The book's key terms. Each term is a heading; an "Also:" line gives the other ways the lessons write it; then the
definition, in one or two sentences a first-year engineering student can use. A term is underlined the first time a page
uses it: point at it to read its meaning.

## Alternative hypothesis
Also: alternative hypotheses, H1
The claim a test looks for evidence of, such as "the mean strength is above 50 MPa". The data support it only when they lead us to reject the null hypothesis.

## Analysis of variance
Also: ANOVA
A test of whether the means of three or more groups differ, made by comparing the variation between the groups with the variation within them.

## Bimodal
A distribution with two peaks, often a sign that the data come from two different sources mixed together.

## Box plot
Also: box plots, boxplot, boxplots
A picture of the five-number summary: a box from the first to the third quartile with a line at the median, whiskers to the data within 1.5 IQR, and points beyond them as outliers.

## Central limit theorem
The rule that the sampling distribution of the sample mean is close to normal for a large enough sample, whatever the shape of the population (as long as its variance is finite), with standard error σ/√n.

## Chi-square test
Also: chi-square tests, chi-squared test
A test that compares counts with the counts expected: goodness of fit to stated proportions, or independence in a table of counts.

## Coefficient of determination
Also: R², R-squared
The share of the variation in the response that a regression explains, from 0 (none) to 1 (all of it).

## Coefficient of variation
Also: CV
The standard deviation divided by the mean, often as a percent: spread compared with size, so two scales can be compared.

## Confidence interval
Also: confidence intervals
An interval calculated from a sample by a method that gives intervals containing the true value in a stated share of repeated samples, such as 95 %.

## Confidence level
Also: confidence levels
The share of samples whose interval would contain the true value, such as 95 %: 1 − α.

## Continuous distribution
Also: continuous distributions, continuous random variable, continuous random variables
A model for a quantity that can take any value in a range, such as a length or a time; probabilities are areas under its density.

## Correlation
Also: correlation coefficient, Pearson correlation
A number from −1 to 1 that says how closely two measurements follow a straight line together; it does not say that one causes the other.

## Critical value
Also: critical values
The value of a test statistic at the edge of the rejection region, from the test's distribution and α.

## Cumulative distribution function
Also: CDF
The probability that a random variable is at most x, P(X ≤ x), for every x.

## Degrees of freedom
Also: df
The number of values free to vary once the estimates in a statistic are fixed, such as n − 1 for a sample variance; it picks which t, χ² or F distribution to use.

## Discrete distribution
Also: discrete distributions, discrete random variable, discrete random variables
A model for a count, such as defects in a batch, that gives the probability of each value.

## Effect size
How big a difference is, apart from whether it is statistically significant: the difference in means itself, or Cohen's d (the difference divided by the standard deviation).

## Expected value
Also: expected values
The long-run average of a random variable: each value times its probability, added up. The population mean.

## Exponential distribution
The continuous distribution of the time between events that happen at a steady rate λ, with mean 1/λ; it has no memory.

## F-test
Also: F test, F statistic
A test that compares two variances (or, in ANOVA and regression, two mean squares) by their ratio.

## Factorial design
Also: factorial designs, factorial experiment
An experiment that runs every combination of the levels of two or more factors, so their interactions can be seen.

## Histogram
Also: histograms
A bar chart of how many values fall in each of a row of equal bins: the shape of the data.

## Hypothesis test
Also: hypothesis tests, hypothesis testing, significance test
A rule for deciding, from a sample, whether the data are strong enough evidence against a null hypothesis.

## Independent events
Two events where knowing one happened does not change the probability of the other: P(A and B) = P(A)·P(B).

## Interquartile range
Also: IQR
The spread of the middle half of the data: the third quartile minus the first.

## Kurtosis
How heavy the tails of a distribution are compared with a normal distribution.

## Least squares
Also: least-squares, ordinary least squares, OLS
The way regression chooses its line: the one that makes the sum of the squared residuals as small as possible.

## Level of significance
Also: significance level, α
The probability of a Type I error that a test allows, often 0.05; reject the null hypothesis when the p-value is below it.

## Margin of error
The half-width of a confidence interval: how far the estimate may be from the true value at the stated confidence.

## Sample mean
Also: sample means, arithmetic mean, average
The sum of the values divided by how many there are; the balance point of the data.

## Median
Also: medians
The middle value when the data are in order (the average of the two middle values for an even count); half the data lie on each side.

## Mode
The value, or the bin, that occurs most often.

## Multiple regression
Also: multiple linear regression
A regression with two or more predictors, each with its own coefficient.

## Nonparametric test
Also: nonparametric tests, nonparametric
A test that does not assume a normal distribution, usually working with ranks (Mann–Whitney, Wilcoxon, Kruskal–Wallis).

## Normal distribution
Also: normal distributions, Gaussian distribution, bell curve
The symmetric, bell-shaped continuous distribution set by its mean μ and standard deviation σ; about 95 % of its values lie within 2σ of μ.

## Null hypothesis
Also: null hypotheses, H0
The claim of no effect or no difference that a test assumes and tries to find evidence against.

## Outlier
Also: outliers
A value far from the rest of the data, often taken as one beyond 1.5 IQR outside the quartiles. Check it before you remove it.

## p-value
Also: p-values, p value, p values
The probability, if the null hypothesis were true, of a result at least as extreme as the one observed. A small p-value says the data would be unusual if the null hypothesis were true.

## Paired samples
Also: paired data, paired t-test, paired t test
Two measurements on the same units (before and after, two gauges on each part); analyse the differences, not two separate samples.

## Parameter
Also: parameters
A number that describes a whole population, such as μ or σ; it is estimated from a sample by a statistic.

## Percentile
Also: percentiles, quantile, quantiles
The value below which a stated share of the data lie: the 90th percentile has 90 % of the data below it.

## Poisson distribution
The discrete distribution of the number of events in a fixed interval when they happen independently at a steady rate λ; its mean and variance are both λ.

## Binomial distribution
The discrete distribution of the number of successes in n independent trials, each with the same probability p.

## Population
Also: populations
Every unit the question is about, such as every bolt a machine will make; usually too many to measure, so a sample is taken.

## Power of a test
Also: statistical power
The probability that a test rejects a false null hypothesis: 1 − β. It grows with the sample size and the size of the effect.

## Prediction interval
Also: prediction intervals
A range that will contain one new observation with a stated probability; wider than the confidence interval for the mean.

## Probability density function
Also: density function
The curve of a continuous distribution; the probability of a range is the area under it.

## Random variable
Also: random variables
A quantity whose value is set by chance, such as the number of defects or a lifetime.

## Regression
Also: linear regression, simple linear regression, regression line
A model that predicts a response from one or more predictors, fitted by least squares.

## Residual
Also: residuals
An observed value minus the value the model predicts for it. Their pattern shows whether the model fits.

## Sample
Also: samples
The units actually measured, chosen from the population; a statistic from it estimates the population's parameter.

## Sampling distribution
Also: sampling distributions
The distribution of a statistic, such as the sample mean, over all the samples that could be taken.

## Skewness
Also: skew, skewed
How lopsided a distribution is; it is named by its long tail (right-skewed has the long tail to the right).

## Standard deviation
Also: standard deviations, sample standard deviation, SD
The typical distance of the values from their mean, in the data's own units: the square root of the variance.

## Standard error
Also: standard errors, SE
The standard deviation of a statistic over repeated samples, such as σ/√n for the mean: how much the estimate wobbles.

## Statistic
Also: sample statistic
A number calculated from a sample, such as x̄ or s, used to estimate a population parameter.

## t-distribution
Also: t distribution, Student's t
The bell-shaped distribution, wider than the normal, used when σ is estimated by s; its shape depends on the degrees of freedom.

## t-test
Also: t-tests, t test, t tests
A test of means that uses the t-distribution because σ is estimated from the data.

## Test statistic
Also: test statistics
The number a test calculates from the sample (z, t, χ², F) and compares with its distribution.

## Type I error
Also: Type I errors
Rejecting a null hypothesis that is true: a false alarm. Its probability is α.

## Type II error
Also: Type II errors
Failing to reject a null hypothesis that is false: a missed effect. Its probability is β.

## Uniform distribution
A distribution where every value in a range is equally likely.

## Variance
Also: variances, sample variance
The average squared distance of the values from their mean (dividing by n − 1 for a sample): the square of the standard deviation.

## Weibull distribution
The flexible continuous distribution of lifetimes and strengths, set by a shape β and a scale η; used in reliability.

## z-score
Also: z-scores, z score, standardized value
How many standard deviations a value is from the mean: (x − μ)/σ.

## Econometrics
Regression and the other methods of statistics applied to economic data, which are usually observational rather than from experiments.

## Price elasticity of demand
Also: elasticity, elasticities, price elasticity
The percentage change in the quantity bought for a 1 % change in price; the slope of a log–log regression of quantity on price. Below −1 is elastic, between −1 and 0 inelastic.

## Omitted-variable bias
Also: omitted variable, omitted variables
The bias in a regression slope when a variable that affects y and moves with x is left out of the model, so x gets the credit for it.

## Time series
A quantity measured at regular times (monthly sales, yearly rainfall), usually made of a trend, a seasonal pattern and noise.

## Seasonality
Also: seasonal pattern, seasonal index
A pattern in a time series that repeats every year, such as more heating in winter.

## Moving average
Also: moving averages
The average of the last k values of a time series, recalculated each period; it smooths out noise and, with k a full year, the season.

## Exponential smoothing
A forecast whose level is a weighted average of the newest value and the previous level, Lₜ = α yₜ + (1 − α) Lₜ₋₁.

## Price index
Also: consumer price index, CPI
The cost of a fixed basket of goods and services relative to a base period set to 100; its percentage change is inflation.

## Inflation
Also: inflation rate
The percentage rise in a price index over a period, usually a year.

## Taylor rule
A description of a central bank's policy rate as a linear function of inflation and the output gap, i = 1 + 1.5π + 0.5y in Taylor's 1993 form.

## Output gap
How far an economy's output is above or below its sustainable (potential) level, as a percentage of potential output.

## Value at Risk
Also: VaR
The loss that will not be exceeded with a stated probability over a stated time; the lower quantile of the change in value, read as a loss.

## Expected shortfall
The average loss on the bad days beyond the Value at Risk.

## Diversification
Lowering a portfolio's risk by holding assets that do not always move together, so some of their ups and downs cancel.

## Beta (finance)
Also: CAPM beta, market beta
The slope of the regression of an asset's excess return on the market's: how much it moves when the market moves 1 %.

## Capital asset pricing model
Also: CAPM
The model in which an asset's expected excess return is its beta times the market's expected excess return.

## Conversion rate
Also: conversion rates
The share of visitors or customers who take the action wanted, such as buying.

## A/B test
Also: A/B tests, A/B testing
An experiment that shows two versions (A and B) to randomly chosen customers and compares an outcome, often with a test of two proportions.

## Clinical trial
Also: clinical trials, randomised trial, randomized trial
An experiment on patients that compares a treatment with a control, assigning patients at random.

## Randomisation
Also: randomization, randomised, randomized
Assigning units to groups by chance, so the groups differ only by chance in everything except the treatment.

## Blinding
Also: double-blind, single-blind
Keeping patients (and in a double-blind trial their doctors and assessors) from knowing which treatment each patient gets.

## Placebo
A dummy treatment given to a control group, so that the comparison is not distorted by expectations.

## Number needed to treat
Also: NNT
The number of patients who must be treated for one more to benefit: 1 divided by the absolute risk difference.

## Prevalence
The share of a population that has a condition at a given time.

## Incidence
The share of people free of a condition who develop it over a stated period.

## Relative risk
Also: risk ratio
The risk of a disease among the exposed divided by the risk among the unexposed.

## Odds ratio
Also: odds ratios
The odds of disease among the exposed divided by the odds among the unexposed, (a·d)/(b·c) in a 2 × 2 table; close to the relative risk for a rare disease.

## Cohort study
Also: cohort studies, cohort
An observational study that follows exposed and unexposed people forward in time and counts who develops the disease.

## Case-control study
Also: case-control studies
An observational study that compares the past exposure of people with a disease (cases) and similar people without it (controls).

## Confounder
Also: confounding, confounders
A third variable related to both the exposure and the outcome, which can make an association look stronger or weaker than it is.

## Sensitivity
The share of people with a condition whom a test correctly flags as positive.

## Specificity
The share of people without a condition whom a test correctly clears as negative.

## Positive predictive value
Also: PPV, predictive value
The probability that a person with a positive test has the condition; it depends on the prevalence (Bayes' rule).

## ROC curve
Also: ROC, area under the curve, AUC
A plot of sensitivity against 1 − specificity for every cut-off of a test; the area under it measures how well the test separates the groups.

## Survival function
Also: survival curve
S(t) = P(T > t), the share still free of the event at time t.

## Hazard
Also: hazard rate
The rate at which the event happens at time t among those who have not yet had it.

## Censoring
Also: censored
Knowing only that a time to an event is longer than the follow-up, because the study ended or the subject left first.

## Kaplan–Meier estimate
Also: Kaplan–Meier, Kaplan-Meier
The step-shaped estimate of a survival function from data with censoring, multiplying (1 − events/at risk) at each event time.

## Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium
Also: Hardy–Weinberg, Hardy-Weinberg
The genotype proportions p², 2pq and q² expected for a gene with allele frequencies p and q in a randomly mating population.

## False discovery rate
Also: FDR, Benjamini–Hochberg
The expected share of false positives among the results declared significant; controlled by the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure.

## LD50
Also: EC50, median lethal dose
The dose (LD50) or concentration (EC50) at which half of those exposed respond; 10^(−b₀/b₁) from a logit line on log dose.

## Logit
Also: logits, log-odds
ln(p/(1 − p)), which turns a probability between 0 and 1 into a number on the whole line.

## Annual maximum
Also: annual maxima, annual peak, annual peaks
The largest value of a quantity (flow, rainfall) in each year; the data of a frequency analysis.

## Exceedance probability
The chance that a year's maximum exceeds a given value, P(X > x); the reciprocal of the return period.

## Return period
Also: recurrence interval, 100-year flood
The average time between exceedances of a value, T = 1/p for annual exceedance probability p; the 100-year flood has a 1 % chance every year.

## Frequency factor
Also: frequency factors
K_T in Q_T = x̄ + K_T·s, which turns the mean and standard deviation of annual maxima into the T-year value for a chosen distribution.

## Gumbel distribution
Also: Gumbel, extreme value type I
A distribution of annual maxima often used for floods and rainfall, with K₁₀₀ = 3.137.

## Log-Pearson Type III
Also: log-Pearson III, LP3
The distribution of flood peaks whose logarithms follow a Pearson Type III distribution; the US standard for flood frequency (Bulletin 17C).

## Mann–Kendall test
Also: Mann–Kendall, Mann-Kendall
A test for a trend that counts rising and falling pairs of values over time, S = Σ sign(xⱼ − xᵢ), without assuming normality.

## Sen's slope
The median of the slopes between every pair of points in a series; a trend estimate robust to unusual values.

## Stationarity
Also: stationary
The assumption that a quantity's distribution does not change over time.

## Lognormal distribution
Also: lognormal, log-normal
A distribution whose logarithm is normal; positive and right-skewed, typical of concentrations and flood peaks.

## Geometric mean
The exponential of the mean of the logarithms; the median of a lognormal distribution.

## Detection limit
Also: detection limits, censored value
The lowest concentration a laboratory can measure reliably; values below it are reported as "less than" the limit.

## Inverse distance weighting
Also: IDW
Interpolation as a weighted average of measured values with weights 1/d², so nearer points count more.

## Variogram
Also: semivariance, semivariogram
The semivariance γ(h) of a spatial quantity plotted against separation distance h; its nugget, sill and range describe how alike nearby places are.

## Nugget
The variogram's value as the distance goes to zero: measurement error and very short-range variation.

## Kriging
Spatial interpolation with weights chosen from a fitted variogram, giving an estimate and its error at every point.

## Gutenberg–Richter law
Also: Gutenberg–Richter, b-value
log₁₀ N = a − b M: the yearly number of earthquakes of at least magnitude M falls tenfold for each magnitude when b = 1.

## Mark–recapture
Also: capture–recapture, Lincoln–Petersen
Estimating a population size by marking animals, recatching a sample and counting the marked ones: N̂ = M·C/R.

## Shannon index
Also: Shannon diversity, diversity index
H = −Σ pᵢ ln pᵢ, a measure of species diversity that grows with the number of species and their evenness.

## Nonresponse bias
Also: nonresponse
The error when the people who answer a survey differ from those who do not; a larger sample does not remove it.

## Stratified sample
Also: stratified sampling, stratification
A sample taken separately from groups (strata) of the population, so that each group is represented.

## Weighting
Also: survey weights, weighted
Counting some survey answers more than others so the sample's mix of groups matches the population's.

## Likert scale
Also: Likert item, Likert items, Likert
A survey question with ordered answers (strongly disagree to strongly agree); several items are added into a scale score.

## Cronbach's alpha
Also: Cronbach alpha, internal consistency
A measure of how consistently the items of a scale measure the same thing; α = k/(k − 1)(1 − Σsᵢ²/s_total²).

## Reliability
Also: test–retest reliability
How consistently a measure gives the same result for the same thing.

## Validity
How well a measure measures what it claims to: content, criterion and construct validity.

## Ceiling effect
Also: floor effect
Many scores at the top (or bottom) of a scale, so it cannot show further change for them.

## Cohen's d
Also: standardized mean difference
The difference between two means in units of their pooled standard deviation; about 0.2 small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large.

## Reverse causation
The association that arises because the supposed effect actually causes the supposed cause.

## Simpson's paradox
An association that appears in every group but reverses (or vanishes) when the groups are combined, because their mix differs.

## Norm group
Also: norms, norming
The large reference group whose scores give the mean and standard deviation against which later scores are compared.

## Standard score
Also: standard scores, T-score
A score rescaled from a z-score to a fixed mean and standard deviation, such as 100 and 15, or 50 and 10 (T-scores).

## Standard error of measurement
Also: SEM
How far an observed test score may be from the person's true score: SD √(1 − reliability).

## Factor analysis
A method that finds a few underlying factors explaining the correlations among many items or tests.

## Replication crisis
Also: replication, reproducibility
The finding, from repeating published studies, that many results in psychology and other fields do not hold up.

## p-hacking
Also: p hacking
Trying many analyses (outcomes, exclusions, covariates, stopping times) and reporting the one with p < 0.05, which inflates false positives.

## Preregistration
Also: preregistered, registered report
Publishing a study's hypotheses, sample size and analysis before collecting the data.

## Publication bias
Also: file-drawer problem
The tendency for significant results to be published and non-significant ones not, which biases the published record.

## Overfitting
Also: overfit, overfits
A model fitting the noise of its training data, so it predicts new data worse than a simpler model would.

## Training set
Also: training data, test set, test data
The data a model is fitted to; the test set is data kept back to measure how well it predicts.

## Cross-validation
Also: k-fold cross-validation, leave-one-out
Estimating a model's prediction error by fitting it on part of the data and predicting the rest, in turn, over several splits.

## Root mean squared error
Also: RMSE
The square root of the mean squared difference between predicted and actual values.

## Data leakage
Also: leakage
Information about the outcome or the test cases slipping into a model's training, so its test error looks better than reality.

## Confusion matrix
The table of a classifier's predictions against the actual classes: true and false positives and negatives.

## Precision (classification)
Also: positive predictive value of a classifier
The share of the cases a classifier flags that are really positive, TP/(TP + FP).

## Recall (classification)
Also: recall of a classifier
The share of the real positives a classifier catches, TP/(TP + FN); sensitivity.

## F1 score
Also: F1
The harmonic mean of precision and recall, 2PR/(P + R).

## Class imbalance
Also: imbalanced classes
One class being much rarer than the other, which makes accuracy a misleading measure.

## Logistic regression
A regression for a yes/no outcome that models the log-odds as a linear function of the predictors; also a classifier.

## Practical significance
Whether a difference is large enough to matter, as against whether it is statistically significant.

## Naive Bayes
A classifier that multiplies the prior odds by each feature's likelihood ratio, assuming the features are independent within each class.

## Likelihood ratio
Also: likelihood ratios
P(evidence | one class)/P(evidence | the other): how many times more likely the evidence is under one class.

## Prior probability
Also: posterior probability
The probability before the evidence (prior) and after it (posterior), linked by Bayes' rule.

## Anomaly detection
Also: anomaly, anomalies
Finding cases that do not fit the pattern of the rest: outliers in the data's terms.

## k-means
Also: k-means clustering
Clustering that assigns each point to the nearest of k centres and moves each centre to the mean of its points, repeatedly.

## Availability
The share of time a system works: MTBF/(MTBF + MTTR).

## Benchmark
Also: benchmarking, benchmarks
Measuring a system's performance on a fixed task, repeated so the result has a confidence interval.

## Sabermetrics
The statistical analysis of baseball, judging players and strategies by the numbers that go with scoring and winning.

## On-base percentage
Also: OBP
The share of plate appearances in which a batter reaches base by a hit, a walk or being hit by a pitch.

## Batting average
Also: AVG
A batter's hits divided by at-bats; it ignores walks.

## Regression to the mean
Also: regression toward the mean
The tendency of an extreme result, partly due to luck, to be followed by one closer to the average.

## Pythagorean expectation
Also: Pythagorean formula
A team's expected win share from points scored (RS) and allowed (RA): RS^k/(RS^k + RA^k).

## Elo rating
Also: Elo, Elo ratings
A rating system in which the rating difference gives each side's expected score, and ratings move toward each result.

## Win probability
The chance that a team wins from a given score and time left, from a model of the rest of the game.

## Hot hand
The belief that a player who has just succeeded is more likely to succeed on the next attempt.

## Runs test
Also: Wald–Wolfowitz runs test
A test of whether a sequence has too few or too many runs (stretches of like outcomes) for independent trials.

## Decimal odds
The total returned per unit staked if an outcome happens; their reciprocal is the implied probability.

## Implied probability
The probability an offered price implies, 1/(decimal odds); the implied probabilities of all outcomes add to more than 1.

## Bookmaker's margin
Also: overround, vig
The amount by which the implied probabilities of all outcomes exceed 1; it makes the expected value of a stake negative.

## Typical error
The noise in one measurement of a test: the standard deviation of trial-to-trial differences divided by √2.

## Smallest worthwhile change
Also: SWC
The smallest change in a measure that matters in practice, often 0.2 × the between-athlete standard deviation.
