---
title: Glossary
---

The book's key terms. Each term is a heading; an "Also:" line gives the other ways the lessons write it; then the
definition, in one or two sentences a first-year engineering student can use. A term is underlined the first time a page
uses it: point at it to read its meaning.

## Alternative hypothesis
Also: alternative hypotheses, H1
The claim a test looks for evidence of, such as "the mean strength is above 50 MPa". The data support it only when they lead us to reject the null hypothesis.

## Analysis of variance
Also: ANOVA
A test of whether the means of three or more groups differ, made by comparing the variation between the groups with the variation within them.

## Bimodal
A distribution with two peaks, often a sign that the data come from two different sources mixed together.

## Box plot
Also: box plots, boxplot, boxplots
A picture of the five-number summary: a box from the first to the third quartile with a line at the median, whiskers to the data within 1.5 IQR, and points beyond them as outliers.

## Central limit theorem
The rule that the sampling distribution of the sample mean is close to normal for a large enough sample, whatever the shape of the population (as long as its variance is finite), with standard error σ/√n.

## Chi-square test
Also: chi-square tests, chi-squared test
A test that compares counts with the counts expected: goodness of fit to stated proportions, or independence in a table of counts.

## Coefficient of determination
Also: R², R-squared
The share of the variation in the response that a regression explains, from 0 (none) to 1 (all of it).

## Coefficient of variation
Also: CV
The standard deviation divided by the mean, often as a percent: spread compared with size, so two scales can be compared.

## Confidence interval
Also: confidence intervals
An interval calculated from a sample by a method that gives intervals containing the true value in a stated share of repeated samples, such as 95 %.

## Confidence level
Also: confidence levels
The share of samples whose interval would contain the true value, such as 95 %: 1 − α.

## Continuous distribution
Also: continuous distributions, continuous random variable, continuous random variables
A model for a quantity that can take any value in a range, such as a length or a time; probabilities are areas under its density.

## Correlation
Also: correlation coefficient, Pearson correlation
A number from −1 to 1 that says how closely two measurements follow a straight line together; it does not say that one causes the other.

## Critical value
Also: critical values
The value of a test statistic at the edge of the rejection region, from the test's distribution and α.

## Cumulative distribution function
Also: CDF
The probability that a random variable is at most x, P(X ≤ x), for every x.

## Degrees of freedom
Also: df
The number of values free to vary once the estimates in a statistic are fixed, such as n − 1 for a sample variance; it picks which t, χ² or F distribution to use.

## Discrete distribution
Also: discrete distributions, discrete random variable, discrete random variables
A model for a count, such as defects in a batch, that gives the probability of each value.

## Effect size
How big a difference is, apart from whether it is statistically significant: the difference in means itself, or Cohen's d (the difference divided by the standard deviation).

## Expected value
Also: expected values
The long-run average of a random variable: each value times its probability, added up. The population mean.

## Exponential distribution
The continuous distribution of the time between events that happen at a steady rate λ, with mean 1/λ; it has no memory.

## F-test
Also: F test, F statistic
A test that compares two variances (or, in ANOVA and regression, two mean squares) by their ratio.

## Factorial design
Also: factorial designs, factorial experiment
An experiment that runs every combination of the levels of two or more factors, so their interactions can be seen.

## Histogram
Also: histograms
A bar chart of how many values fall in each of a row of equal bins: the shape of the data.

## Hypothesis test
Also: hypothesis tests, hypothesis testing, significance test
A rule for deciding, from a sample, whether the data are strong enough evidence against a null hypothesis.

## Independent events
Two events where knowing one happened does not change the probability of the other: P(A and B) = P(A)·P(B).

## Interquartile range
Also: IQR
The spread of the middle half of the data: the third quartile minus the first.

## Kurtosis
How heavy the tails of a distribution are compared with a normal distribution.

## Least squares
Also: least-squares, ordinary least squares, OLS
The way regression chooses its line: the one that makes the sum of the squared residuals as small as possible.

## Level of significance
Also: significance level, α
The probability of a Type I error that a test allows, often 0.05; reject the null hypothesis when the p-value is below it.

## Margin of error
The half-width of a confidence interval: how far the estimate may be from the true value at the stated confidence.

## Sample mean
Also: sample means, arithmetic mean, average
The sum of the values divided by how many there are; the balance point of the data.

## Median
Also: medians
The middle value when the data are in order (the average of the two middle values for an even count); half the data lie on each side.

## Mode
The value, or the bin, that occurs most often.

## Multiple regression
Also: multiple linear regression
A regression with two or more predictors, each with its own coefficient.

## Nonparametric test
Also: nonparametric tests, nonparametric
A test that does not assume a normal distribution, usually working with ranks (Mann–Whitney, Wilcoxon, Kruskal–Wallis).

## Normal distribution
Also: normal distributions, Gaussian distribution, bell curve
The symmetric, bell-shaped continuous distribution set by its mean μ and standard deviation σ; about 95 % of its values lie within 2σ of μ.

## Null hypothesis
Also: null hypotheses, H0
The claim of no effect or no difference that a test assumes and tries to find evidence against.

## Outlier
Also: outliers
A value far from the rest of the data, often taken as one beyond 1.5 IQR outside the quartiles. Check it before you remove it.

## p-value
Also: p-values, p value, p values
The probability, if the null hypothesis were true, of a result at least as extreme as the one observed. A small p-value says the data would be unusual if the null hypothesis were true.

## Paired samples
Also: paired data, paired t-test, paired t test
Two measurements on the same units (before and after, two gauges on each part); analyse the differences, not two separate samples.

## Parameter
Also: parameters
A number that describes a whole population, such as μ or σ; it is estimated from a sample by a statistic.

## Percentile
Also: percentiles, quantile, quantiles
The value below which a stated share of the data lie: the 90th percentile has 90 % of the data below it.

## Poisson distribution
The discrete distribution of the number of events in a fixed interval when they happen independently at a steady rate λ; its mean and variance are both λ.

## Binomial distribution
The discrete distribution of the number of successes in n independent trials, each with the same probability p.

## Population
Also: populations
Every unit the question is about, such as every bolt a machine will make; usually too many to measure, so a sample is taken.

## Power of a test
Also: statistical power
The probability that a test rejects a false null hypothesis: 1 − β. It grows with the sample size and the size of the effect.

## Prediction interval
Also: prediction intervals
A range that will contain one new observation with a stated probability; wider than the confidence interval for the mean.

## Probability density function
Also: density function
The curve of a continuous distribution; the probability of a range is the area under it.

## Random variable
Also: random variables
A quantity whose value is set by chance, such as the number of defects or a lifetime.

## Regression
Also: linear regression, simple linear regression, regression line
A model that predicts a response from one or more predictors, fitted by least squares.

## Residual
Also: residuals
An observed value minus the value the model predicts for it. Their pattern shows whether the model fits.

## Sample
Also: samples
The units actually measured, chosen from the population; a statistic from it estimates the population's parameter.

## Sampling distribution
Also: sampling distributions
The distribution of a statistic, such as the sample mean, over all the samples that could be taken.

## Skewness
Also: skew, skewed
How lopsided a distribution is; it is named by its long tail (right-skewed has the long tail to the right).

## Standard deviation
Also: standard deviations, sample standard deviation, SD
The typical distance of the values from their mean, in the data's own units: the square root of the variance.

## Standard error
Also: standard errors, SE
The standard deviation of a statistic over repeated samples, such as σ/√n for the mean: how much the estimate wobbles.

## Statistic
Also: sample statistic
A number calculated from a sample, such as x̄ or s, used to estimate a population parameter.

## t-distribution
Also: t distribution, Student's t
The bell-shaped distribution, wider than the normal, used when σ is estimated by s; its shape depends on the degrees of freedom.

## t-test
Also: t-tests, t test, t tests
A test of means that uses the t-distribution because σ is estimated from the data.

## Test statistic
Also: test statistics
The number a test calculates from the sample (z, t, χ², F) and compares with its distribution.

## Type I error
Also: Type I errors
Rejecting a null hypothesis that is true: a false alarm. Its probability is α.

## Type II error
Also: Type II errors
Failing to reject a null hypothesis that is false: a missed effect. Its probability is β.

## Uniform distribution
A distribution where every value in a range is equally likely.

## Variance
Also: variances, sample variance
The average squared distance of the values from their mean (dividing by n − 1 for a sample): the square of the standard deviation.

## Weibull distribution
The flexible continuous distribution of lifetimes and strengths, set by a shape β and a scale η; used in reliability.

## z-score
Also: z-scores, z score, standardized value
How many standard deviations a value is from the mean: (x − μ)/σ.
