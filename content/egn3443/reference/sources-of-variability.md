# Sources of variability and process quality

## Where variation comes from

| Source | Examples |
|---|---|
| Material | differences between coils, batches, lots, suppliers |
| Machine | tool wear, spindle temperature, vibration, a worn fixture |
| Method | different set-ups, procedures, sequences |
| Measurement | gauge repeatability, reading errors, calibration |
| People | different operators, training, fatigue |
| Environment | temperature, humidity, dust, time of day |

## Two kinds of cause

| | Common cause | Special (assignable) cause |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | many small effects always present | a specific event outside the normal process |
| How it looks | steady random spread around a stable mean | an outlier, a sudden shift, a trend, a change in spread |
| What to do | change the process itself to reduce it | find the cause and remove it |
| Example | small variation in bar hardness | a chipped tool, a wrong setting, a bad batch |

A process with only common-cause variation is **stable** (in statistical control): its future behavior can be predicted.

## Spread and quality

A part is defective when it is outside its tolerance, so the share of defects depends on the spread as well as the mean. With the mean centered between the limits, the larger the standard deviation compared with the tolerance, the more parts fall outside.
