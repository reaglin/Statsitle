Engineers measure things that vary. No two concrete cylinders break at the same load, no two bolts are exactly the same length, and no two days bring the same rain. Statistics is the set of tools for deciding what to do when the numbers vary: whether a batch meets its specification, whether a machine has drifted, how likely a part is to fail.

This module is a tour of the whole course. It shows each of the big ideas once, on engineering data, so you know where the course is going:

- **describing data** with a few numbers and two pictures (Module 2 goes deeper);
- **variability**: where it comes from, and why it is the number engineers care about most;
- **probability distributions**, the models for random quantities (Modules 4 and 5);
- **inference**: confidence intervals and hypothesis tests, which reach conclusions about a whole process from a sample (Modules 7 to 10);
- **quality control**, where all of these meet on the factory floor.

Items 1.4 and 1.5 are previews. They show what a distribution, a confidence interval and a hypothesis test give you and how to read them, and their exercises are questions on reading and choosing. The later modules teach how each is calculated.
