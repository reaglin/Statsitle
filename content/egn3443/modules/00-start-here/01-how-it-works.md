# How Statistics+ works

Statistics+ has two parts in one window, and the two buttons at the top switch between them:

- the **Course** (Ctrl+L): the lessons, module by module, with your progress;
- the **Workbench** (Ctrl+B): the statistics calculator, for your own data and your assignments.

You are in the Course now.

## The list on the left

The list on the left is the whole course, in order.

- **My progress** is at the top. It shows every module, the module checks, the outcomes to review, and the time you have spent.
- Then come the **modules**: 0 Start here, 1 Statistics for Engineers, 2 Descriptive Statistics, and so on. Choose a module to see what it teaches (its outcomes) and its items.
- Under each module are its **items**. Each item is one topic of the course, numbered by module: 1.2 is the second item of Module 1.

Each item has a mark that shows how far you are:

| Mark | Means |
|---|---|
| ✓ | complete |
| ◐ | started, not yet complete |
| ○ | not started |

## The four tabs of an item

Every item has the same four tabs. Use them in order.

| Tab | Keys | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **Learn** | Ctrl+1 | The lesson: the idea, the formula, a small case worked by hand, and the Python and R |
| **Example** | Ctrl+2 | A worked analysis on an engineering dataset: the data, every result, the figures and the code |
| **Exercise** | Ctrl+3 | Your turn: a new dataset to work, or questions to answer |
| **Reference** | Ctrl+4 | The formulas, the Python and R functions, and the sources, to look back at |

The Example uses a dataset like the Exercise's, never the same one. It shows the method; the Exercise checks that you can use it.

## Doing an exercise

An exercise is one of two kinds.

1. **A dataset to work.** Calculate the results asked for: by hand, in Python or R, or in the Workbench. Type each one in its box and press **Check my answers**. Give the decimals the exercise asks for; when it does not say, two decimals are usually enough, and counts must be exact. A wrong answer says whether it is too high or too low, or names the common mistake it matches, never what the answer is. **Show a hint** gives one hint at a time.
2. **Questions.** Choose an answer, type a number or a word, and check. The questions on an item's Exercise tab are practice: after a wrong try they show the right answer and why. A **module check** at the end of each module does not; you need 80 % to pass it, and you can try again.

An item is **complete** when you pass its exercise or its questions. An item with neither, a reading, is complete when you press **I have read this**.

## The Workbench

The Workbench is the calculator. Choose an analysis on the left (Descriptive statistics, Confidence interval, One-sample test, …), type or paste the data, and the results appear at once. Its tabs are **Properties** (the data and the results), **Report**, **Plots**, **Code** (the Python and R) and **Instructions**.

On an Example tab, **Open in the Workbench** copies the example there, so you can change the numbers and watch the results move. **Course** (Ctrl+L) brings you back to the item you were on.

Use the Workbench to check your hand calculations, not to skip them. The module checks ask you to calculate small cases yourself.

## Your progress

Your progress is kept in a file of your own, locked with a PIN you choose.

- **Signing in.** When Statistics+ starts, the **Who is learning?** screen asks for your name and PIN. The first time, press **New learner**, type your name and choose a PIN of 4 to 6 digits. Statistics+ then gives you a **recovery code**. Write it down or print it: it is the only way to set a new PIN if you forget yours.
- **Saving.** Your progress is saved after every step. There is no Save button to remember.
- **The Learner menu.** **Learner ▸ Sign in…** opens Who is learning? again, **Learner ▸ Sign out** saves and locks your file, and **Learner ▸ Back up my progress…** saves a copy.
- **Backing up.** Lab computers are wiped and replaced, so back up your progress to a USB drive or a cloud folder, often. On another computer, **Add learner from file** on the Who is learning? screen brings it in, and two copies of the same learner are combined, so nothing is lost.
- **Leaving.** Closing Statistics+ saves and locks your file. On a shared computer it also locks itself after a while without use (15 minutes, unless Settings says otherwise).

If you only want the calculator, **Just the Workbench** on the Who is learning? screen opens it without signing in. Nothing is then recorded as progress.

## When you are stuck

- **F1** (Help ▸ Help for this screen) explains the screen you are on. In the Course it shows how the Course works; in the Workbench it opens the Instructions for the analysis you have chosen.
- **Help ▸ Help on the web** opens the Statistics+ help on softwareplus.ai.
- Hold the mouse over a button or a box to see what it does.

## What to do next

Answer the questions on the **Exercise** tab. They are about finding your way around, and you can try them as often as you like. Then press **Next ▸** for Python and R.
