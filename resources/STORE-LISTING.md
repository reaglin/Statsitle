## Description  [10000]

```
Statistics+ is a free PlusBook for probability and statistics for engineers: a textbook that is
also a program. Read a lesson, try its examples (change the data and watch the figure and the
results move), work the exercise and have it checked, and keep your own notes beside the page.
The statistics calculator, the Workbench, is built in.

(Statistics+ was called Statistle. The calculator is still here, as the Workbench, and opens your
.statistle projects as before.)


A BOOK IN THREE PARTS

Part I - Fundamental Statistics for Engineers. Follows EGN3443, Probability and Statistics for
Engineers, topic by topic in fourteen modules: introduction to statistics for engineers;
descriptive statistics; probability; discrete and continuous distributions; sampling
distributions and the central limit theorem; estimation; hypothesis testing; two-sample tests;
simple, multiple and validating regression; and analysis of variance.

Part II - Advanced Statistics. In development: advanced regression and experimental design,
stochastic processes, multivariate and reliability analysis, Bayesian and computational
statistics. Its modules are in the path, so you can see where they fit.

Part III - Additional Applications of Statistics. How statistics is used in six fields, with each
field's own questions and vocabulary:
  - Biology and Medicine: clinical trials, epidemiology, diagnostic tests, survival analysis,
    genetics, dose-response
  - Business and Economics: econometrics, forecasting, monetary policy, risk, asset pricing,
    market research and A/B tests
  - Environmental and Earth Sciences: floods and rainfall, climate trends, water and air quality,
    spatial statistics, earthquakes, ecological sampling
  - Social Sciences and Psychology: polls, measurement, experiments, causation, psychometrics,
    the replication crisis
  - Data Science and Computing: training and testing models, classification, online
    experiments, naive Bayes, anomaly detection, system performance
  - Sports: sabermetrics, the Pythagorean expectation, ratings and win probability, the hot hand,
    odds as probability, sports science
Each module points back to the Part I topic that teaches a method, and stands on its own.


EVERY TOPIC

  - Learn: the idea in plain words, with figures, key terms underlined (point at one for its
    meaning, click it for the Glossary) and the Python and R
  - Examples: examples to try, each with a figure that moves as you change the data, then the
    worked example with every result
  - Exercise: a new case for you to work; type your answers and Statistics+ checks each one
    against the value the analysis calculates, and names the common mistake when you make it
  - Reference: the formulas, the Python and R, and where to read more
  - My notes: your own notes on any paragraph, kept with your progress

Every module ends with a module check. My progress shows what you have done, what is left,
which outcomes you have mastered and what to review.


THE WINDOW

PlusBook Tools come first in the path: My progress, Hand in a report, My notes, the Glossary
and the Workbench. Each part of the book folds and opens on its own. Help sits on the right of
every screen and folds away (F1). Course Links keeps your course website and your class in the
learning system one click away. The start screen is in sections: Who is learning?, Course Tools
(no sign-in needed), Learner files, and Help and information.


LEARNERS AND REPORTS

Several students can share one computer: each has a progress file locked with a PIN, with a
recovery code if the PIN is forgotten, and backs it up to a drive or cloud folder of their own.

When an instructor asks, a student makes a progress report: a signed PDF of a module check or of
the whole course so far, with the scores, the outcomes, and the results of each exercise passed,
with its figure. A one-line result can be pasted where the instructor asks. Instructors check a
report or a line in the same program (File > Check reports and class codes), with no account and
nothing sent anywhere.


THE WORKBENCH

  - Descriptive statistics, with histogram, fitted normal curve, box plot and control limits
  - Probability rules for two events
  - Discrete and continuous distributions, including Weibull, and a fit to a data column
  - Sampling distributions, and a seeded Simulation
  - Confidence intervals and the sample size for a margin of error
  - One- and two-sample tests: t (pooled, Welch, paired), proportions, variances, with
    Shapiro-Wilk, Levene, Mann-Whitney and Wilcoxon
  - Chi-square tests of goodness of fit and independence
  - Simple and multiple regression, with transformations, intervals, forward and backward
    selection, k-fold and hold-out validation, VIF, leverage and Cook's distance
  - One-way ANOVA with Tukey HSD, Welch's ANOVA, Bartlett, Levene and Kruskal-Wallis; two-way
    ANOVA with interaction

Every analysis explains every input and result in a help column beside it, has a manual page,
makes a report (text, HTML or PDF) with its plots, and writes the Python (numpy, scipy,
statsmodels) and the R that reproduce it. Statistics+ does not run the code and does not need
Python or R installed.


PRIVACY

Statistics+ works offline, collects nothing and sends nothing. Learners' progress and notes stay
on the computer; a report leaves it only as a file the learner saves and hands in. It goes
online only when you ask: Check for a new version asks the Microsoft Store, and Help on the web
and Send feedback open a page in your browser.


FREE

Free, with no advertising, no accounts and no in-app purchases. Created by Dr. Ron Eaglin for the
course it follows. Tell us what you think with Help > Send feedback.
```
